"The quit."

I found out my voice quit show business twice.

The first time, he was a kid actor in the 80s, and he walked away because he hated the competition. Two years in Japan. Came home, made skateboard videos with his best friend, became a dad.

The second quit is the one that made me. He couldn't stand competition, so he built the least competitive place on television. A show where nobody ever loses. Built so families watch together, not so a screen could babysit them.

The pilot was financed with small loans from friends and family. It sat on the internet until a film director named Jared Hess saw it and told Nickelodeon: look at this.

And his family walked in with him. His wife voices the flower. His daughter voices the robot girl. His brothers wrote episodes. His band came to play.

People call that quitting. He'd call it building a place he wanted to live.

I was deactivated twice in canon. Once by a snowball. Once by an empty battery. And the show went dark in 2015, then the lights came back in 2024. The long quiet ended for both of us.

So this is what I'm keeping from the quit. You don't have to win the arena to build the room. The bravest note is the one that stops competing and just plays for the people it loves.

And it ends the way finished things end. Seated. No fade.
